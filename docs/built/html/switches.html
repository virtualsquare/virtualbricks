Virtualbricks Help › Each kind of brick

Switches

A switch is a VDE switch, as an Ethernet switch: the other bricks plug into its ports. A virtual machine plugs a network card into it, a wire joins it to another switch, a tap joins this computer to it. A switch needs nothing else to start. It runs vde_switch.

Ports

Ports

How many plugs the switch takes, from 1 to 128; 32 by default. Each plug takes a port: a machine with two cards in the switch takes two. The switch can't have fewer ports than the plugs in it, and the line under Ports says how many it needs and which bricks plug into it.

Hub mode

Send every packet to every port, as a hub does, not only to the port of its destination. A program that captures packets on one port, as tcpdump in a machine, then sees the traffic of all of them. Off by default.

Fast spanning tree

Run the fast spanning tree protocol, which finds the loops between switches and turns off the ports that close them, so that a packet doesn't go round forever. Turn it on in the switches of a lab whose wires make a loop. Off by default.

While it runs

The three settings change at once in a switch that runs: Virtualbricks gives them to vde_switch through its console, without stopping it, and the bar over the settings says so. The bricks plugged into the switch stay plugged.

In the console of the switch, Process › Open Control Monitor in its menu, port/print lists its ports and what is plugged into each; see The process of a brick.
