§1At a glance

Controls
A dark translucent bar floats over the top right corner, like the Readme tab’s buttons: zoom out, the zoom level, zoom in, fit all, and a menu with the layout and the export (T1, T2, T7).
Zoom
Zoom in and out go through fixed levels from 10% to 400%. The level button goes back to 100%. Fit all shows the whole lab, never above 100%. The tab opens fitted and stays fitted until you zoom (T3, T4, T5).
Mouse, keys
Ctrl and the wheel zoom around the pointer, and a pinch on a touchpad too. Dragging the background moves the picture. Ctrl++, Ctrl+−, Ctrl+0 and F (T6).
Drawing
Graphviz only places the bricks, and Virtualbricks draws them with cairo. Zooming is instant, the picture follows the dark theme, and stopped bricks are grey as in the Bricks list (T8, T9).
Export
PNG, SVG or PDF, at 100% and on white, whatever the zoom and the theme (T10).

§2The tab today

  • A row of controls takes the top of the page: Export as Image and the two directions of the layout.
  • The picture is a PNG that Graphviz writes in the runtime folder, at one size: there is no zoom, and a lab of a dozen bricks scrolls both ways.
  • It is white in the dark theme, and it shows every brick in colour, running or not.
  • A click picks the brick whose centre is within 50 pixels of it. Two close bricks can both claim the click; the first one listed wins.
  • The export copies that PNG under the name you give: lab.svg is a PNG file.
  • The drawing code takes a scale, but the tab always passes 1.0, and a scale below 1 would fail: it resizes the PNG with fractions of a pixel.

§4The zoom

Levels

Zoom in and zoom out step through fixed levels, the ones most applications use (T3):

  • 10%
  • 25%
  • 33%
  • 50%
  • 67%
  • 80%
  • 100%
  • 125%
  • 150%
  • 200%
  • 300%
  • 400%

From a level in between, as after fitting, they go to the next one up or down: from a fitted 75%, zoom in gives 80% and zoom out 67%. Below 10% the names can’t be read, so 10% is the floor, for fitting too: a lab too big for 10% scrolls.

Fit all

Fit all picks the zoom that shows the whole lab with a margin of 20 pixels, and centres it. It never goes above 100%: a lab of three bricks stays at its natural size, in the middle of the tab (T4).

Fitting is a mode. While the fit button is pressed, the picture fits again when the tab changes size, and when a brick is added, removed or connected. Any other zoom releases it. The tab opens fitted, and so does every project (T5).

Where it zooms from

The buttons and the keys keep the centre of the view in place. Ctrl and the wheel, and a pinch, keep the point under the pointer in place, as maps do.

The keys work while the picture has the focus; a click on it gives it the focus. The bricks keep their mouse actions of today: the right button opens a brick’s menu and a double click starts or stops it. Only a drag that starts on the background moves the picture.

Each button has a tooltip and an accessible name, since it shows no word: Zoom Out, Zoom to 100%, Zoom In, Fit All, and More for the menu.

What this machine says: after layout(), pygraphviz gives every brick’s centre and size and every line’s curve, in points, and the size of the whole lab. Laying out four bricks took 165 ms with Graphviz 2.42.4. With B that happens once when the lab changes, not at each zoom step. The brick icons are 96-pixel PNGs: sharp up to 100%, softer above. Pillow is only used to read the size of today’s PNG.

What B draws (T9)

  • Each brick’s icon, and its name below it in the theme’s text colour. A stopped brick’s icon is grey and faded, as in the Bricks list.
  • The links as Graphviz’s curves, in the text colour at 60%.
  • The brick under the pointer on a light disc of the accent colour, with the hand pointer and a tooltip: its name, type and status, as the Bricks list shows them.
  • A click hits a brick exactly, by its icon and its name, at any zoom.
  • No files in the runtime folder: the three that Graphviz writes today go.

§6Implementation plan

With the answers of §8. Each step is a commit with its tests, stopping for review after each.

  1. The layout. A module without GTK, virtualbricks/topology.py: from the bricks and the direction, Graphviz’s places in points: each brick’s centre and size, each link’s curve, the size of the lab. The links follow today’s rules for plugs and sockets.
  2. The view. virtualbricks/gui/mainwindow/topologyview.py: a GtkDrawingArea in a scrolled window that draws a layout at a zoom, with the theme’s colours. It keeps the levels, fit all and its mode, the brick at a point, the hover and its tooltip, Ctrl and the wheel, the pinch, the drag and the keys.
  3. The tab. TopologyTab puts the view under an overlay with the bar and its menu, shows the hint of an empty project, and keeps its rules of today: it draws when it shows and again when a brick changes.
  4. The export. Cairo writes PNG, SVG or PDF, chosen by the file’s extension and offered as filters in the file chooser, at 100% and in the light colours.
  5. The rest. graphics.Topology and Node go, with the temporary files; Pillow leaves pyproject.toml.

§7Tests

  • The layout: bricks and links for each kind of plug, both directions, an empty project; with the Graphviz of the machine.
  • The view: the levels up and down from any zoom, the floor and the ceiling, fit all and its mode after a resize and a change, the brick at a point at several zooms, the hover, the zoom around the pointer, the colours of a stopped brick and of the dark theme.
  • The tab: the bar’s buttons and what they show, the menu, the keys, the hint, and the bricks’ menu and double click.
  • The export: a PNG, an SVG and a PDF, each a file of its kind at the lab’s size.

As before, each new test is checked against the code it guards, broken on a copy.
