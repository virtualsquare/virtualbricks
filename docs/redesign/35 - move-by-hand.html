§1At a glance

Move
Drag a brick, as for a connection, and release it over free room: it stays there. A faded copy of the brick at the end of the line shows where it will go. Over a brick it connects to, the drag still connects (§3, M1).
Line up
Near the column or the row of another brick, the brick lines up with it, and a thin line shows it (§3, M6).
By hand
The first move gives every brick the place it has in the picture. The Layout of the menu then says By Hand, and Graphviz no longer moves the bricks. Left to Right and Top to Bottom lay the lab out again, and the places go (§4, M3).
Others
A brick without a place waits in a row under the lab until it’s moved. The links are straight lines (§4, M4, M5).
Project
Each brick’s table in project.toml has its place: the middle of its icon, in points. Rename, Delete, archives and the windows of --connect keep it (§5, M2, M7).
Tests
Unit tests, a real mouse on Xvfb, and one scenario that moves a brick and finds its place in project.toml (§6, M8).
Plan
Three steps: the places in the project, the layout by hand, the move (§7).

§2Today

  • The tab lays the lab out with Graphviz when it shows, and again after every change of a brick: added, removed, renamed, connected, started or stopped. dot puts the bricks in ranks by their links, left to right or top to bottom, so one new link can move every brick.
  • The direction chosen in the menu belongs to the tab: every start of Virtualbricks has Left to Right again. Nothing about the picture is saved, neither in the project nor in the state of the workspace.
  • A brick pressed and moved drags a line that connects it (page 34); a release over free room does nothing. A press on the background moves the picture.
  • project.toml has a table for each brick: its type, its settings, and its connections in connect, endpoints or nics. The windows of --connect get these tables from the other Virtualbricks in Changed, and their copy of the project reads them (page 19).
  • The projects of 2.1 have no places either: its picture was Graphviz’s too.

§3The move

As M1 A decides, the drag of page 34 does both. What it does today stays: the drag threshold, the line from the brick, the solid line with both discs and the words over a brick it connects to, the scroll near the edges, the zoom during the drag, and Escape.

What’s new: while the pointer is over free room, with no brick under it, a faded copy of the brick, its icon on a disc, sits at the end of the dashed line, where the brick will go. The copy is held where the brick was pressed, so the brick doesn’t jump to put its middle under the pointer. Release there, and the brick moves; its links follow it, and the copy and the line go.

Over a brick it doesn’t connect to, the copy hides and a release does nothing, as today: a brick is never dropped onto another. Its box may still overlap another’s a little; where it goes is your choice.

Lining up

As M6 C decides: when the middle of the copy’s icon comes within 8 pixels of another brick’s column, the copy takes that brick’s x, and a thin line in the selection colour runs through both. A row works the same way, with y. In each direction, the nearest brick wins. The 8 pixels are on the screen, so lining up is just as easy at any zoom. Places are whole points.

After the release

  • Zoomed in or out, not fitted: the bricks that didn’t move stay where they were on the screen, even when the lab grows to the left or upwards, because the scroll follows the corner of the lab.
  • Fitted: the picture fits again, as after any change (page 6, T5).
  • Beyond the picture: the scroll near the edges stops at the end of the picture, so one move takes a brick at most to its edge. After that drop the lab is larger, and the next drag can go further.

§4By hand or by Graphviz

A lab is laid out in one of two ways: by Graphviz, as today, or by hand, when its bricks have places. The menu of the bar says which, as M3 A decides: Layout offers Left to Right, Top to Bottom and By Hand.

  • The first move in a lab laid out by Graphviz gives every brick the place the picture shows, and the moved brick the place where it was released, so nothing else moves. The menu switches to By Hand. Choosing By Hand does the same without a move: each brick keeps the place it has in the picture, and stays there from then on.
  • Left to Right or Top to Bottom, in a lab laid out by hand, take the places away from every brick at once, and Graphviz lays the lab out as today. Since revision 3 they ask nothing first.
  • Graphviz doesn’t run while every brick has a place: after a change, the tab redraws the picture at once.
  • A brick without a place in a lab laid out by hand waits in a row under the lab, as M4 A decides. This can be a new brick, a copy made by Duplicate, a brick made from the console, or one whose place couldn’t be read. The row starts at the left, an inch below the lowest brick (Graphviz’s gap between ranks), in the order of the Bricks list. A brick has no place until it’s moved: a move takes it out of the row, and the bricks after it close up.
  • The links are straight, as M5 A decides: from the edge of one box to the edge of the other, aimed at their middles, in today’s colour. Two links between the same two bricks are drawn as one, as today.
  • Export as Image draws the lab as the tab shows it, by hand too.
  • The direction still belongs to the tab, as today; keeping it in the project is left for later (§9).

§5The places in the project

As M2 A decides, each brick’s table in project.toml holds its place, after its connections:

[bricks.tap1]
# A tap interface of the host, plugged into a switch
type = "tap"
…
# The socket it's plugged into
connect = "sw1"
# Where it is in the picture of the Topology tab,
# laid out by hand: the middle of its icon, x and y
# in points, y going down (default empty)
place = [32, 230]
  • The middle of the icon, not of the box: the box widens with the name and the font, but the icon doesn’t. Places are points of the picture at 100%, whole numbers, with y going down. Where (0, 0) is doesn’t matter: the picture starts at the box around all the bricks, so a lab moved as a whole is the same lab.
  • place = [] means no place. Every brick has the key, since every value is written.
  • Reading is lenient: a place that isn’t [], or isn’t two whole numbers between −105 and 105, is reported, and the brick has no place. A missing key means no place and isn’t reported: the format 1 files written before this step don’t have it.
  • Rename keeps the place, since the brick’s table moves with its name. Delete takes it away. The copy made by Duplicate has no place (§4). An archive carries project.toml, so Export Project and Import keep the picture. A 2.1 project is migrated without places.
  • In memory: Brick.place is None or a Point(x, y), a named tuple. engine.place(places) takes a dict of bricks to places or None: it sets them all, then each brick says it changed, so the windows, the copy of --connect and the autosave follow.
  • The windows of --connect (M7 A): Place, a new command of protocol 2, carries the places as JSON. The other Virtualbricks sets them through its engine and pushes the bricks’ tables in Changed, and the copy reads place from them. Both run the same version (page 19, R11), so the other always knows Place; a refusal, as of a brick deleted there meanwhile, goes to the log: “Cannot move vm1”.
  • The console gets no command: a place belongs to the picture.

§6Tests

  • The layout (test_topology.py): a lab laid out from its places, with boxes around the middles of the icons, straight links cut at the boxes, and the size and corner of the lab; the row of the bricks without a place; no Graphviz when every brick has a place.
  • The project file (test_projectfile.py): the place written and read, [] for none, its note, a wrong place reported, a missing key not reported. Rename, Delete and Duplicate (test_brickfactory.py).
  • The engine and --connect: engine.place(); the Place command; the copy reading place.
  • The view (test_topologyview.py): the copy over free room, hidden over a brick; the release that moves, with a signal that gives the brick and its place; no move after Escape, after a lost grab, or over a brick; lining up in each direction; the bricks that didn’t move staying in place on the screen.
  • The picture (test_picture.py): the copy and the guide line drawn.
  • The tab (test_topology.py of the windows): the first move gives every brick its place; By Hand; Left to Right and Top to Bottom, which take the places away; the menu after opening a project with places.
  • End to end (M8 A): one scenario in topology.feature. A project whose sw1 and sw2 have places; sw2 dragged 100 pixels to the right at 100%; the project saved; project.toml has sw2 100 points further right. The step finds a brick on the screen from its place and from the rules by which the view places the lab (centred under the bar, at 100%), not from pixels.
  • Each new test fails when the code it guards is broken, checked one break at a time. After step 3, a real mouse on Xvfb in the whole application.

§8Decisions

QuestionOptionsDecided
M1 How a brick is moved

A The drag of page 34 moves too. Over free room, a faded copy of the brick sits at the end of the line, and a release moves the brick there; over a brick it connects to, it connects, as today (boards A1, A2). One gesture and nothing new to learn; but a release over free room, which did nothing until now, moves the brick.

B The brick itself follows the pointer, its links stretching, like an icon on a desktop. Dropped on a brick it connects to, it connects and goes back (board B). The line of page 34 is gone.

C Two gestures: a drag from the brick moves it, as in B; a drag from a knob on its disc connects, with the line of page 34 (board C).

D Shift: a drag with Shift held moves the brick, as in B; without Shift, it connects, as today. Nothing on the screen shows this; only the guide and a tooltip can.

 A
M2 Where the places are kept

A In each brick’s table in project.toml, place = [x, y], the middle of its icon (§5). Rename, Delete, archives and the copy of --connect follow with no extra code.

B In a table of the picture, [picture], keyed by brick name. The bricks’ tables stay their settings; but Rename and Delete must mend it, and the copy needs a push of its own.

C In the state of the workspace, state.toml. That is outside the project, so an archive leaves the places behind.

 A
M3 By hand or by Graphviz

A Layout in the menu gets a third choice, By Hand, on while a brick has a place. Left to Right and Top to Bottom take the places away and lay the lab out with Graphviz (board M, §4). Decided with a question first; since revision 3, without one.

B The same, but the places stay in the project while Graphviz lays the lab out, so By Hand brings them back without a question; the choice is kept in the project’s settings. A move in a lab laid out by Graphviz takes the picture’s places anew, and the old places are lost then.

C No By Hand: the first move places every brick, and the menu has the commands Arrange Left to Right and Arrange Top to Bottom, which give every brick Graphviz’s place. Once a brick has moved, the lab is never laid out again after a change.

 A
M4 Where a brick without a place goes, in a lab laid out by hand

A In a row under the lab, from the left, in the order of the Bricks list, with no place until it’s moved (board N).

B Where Graphviz’s neato puts it, with every other brick pinned: near the bricks it links to. A new brick links to none, so it could land anywhere.

C In the middle of what the tab shows, at the first free room around it, and given that place at once.

 A
M5 The links of a lab laid out by hand

A Straight lines from box to box, aimed at the middles (board A2); a move redraws them at once.

B Graphviz’s curves around the bricks (neato -n2 with splines), after each move. A move can bend links far from the brick moved. Checked today with Graphviz 2.42.4: a lab of five bricks takes under a millisecond.

 A
M6 Lining up

A None: a brick goes exactly where it’s released.

B An invisible grid of 16 points. Every place is on it, Graphviz’s too at the first move, which shifts them by up to 8 points.

C Guides: within 8 pixels of another brick’s column or row, the copy lines up with it, with a thin line through both (board G).

 C

B They show the places of the other Virtualbricks, and their drags only connect: a release over free room does nothing there.

§9Later

  • Keep the direction of the layout in the project, alongside the places.
  • Move several bricks at once: a selection, framed by a drag on the background with Shift, or picked with Ctrl and clicks.
  • Move a brick with the keyboard: the arrows, on a brick with the focus.
  • Undo a move with Ctrl+Z.
  • Graphviz’s curves around the bricks in a lab laid out by hand, as a choice (M5 B).
  • Tidy a lab laid out by hand: Graphviz places only the bricks of the row, near the bricks they link to.
