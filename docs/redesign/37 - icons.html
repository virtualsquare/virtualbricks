New icons for the bricks

The icons of the bricks date from 2013, mix four styles, and some of them are hard to read at the sizes the windows show them. This page draws a new set in three styles and shows each in the real windows, light and dark, with running and stopped bricks. It also redraws the logo, with its V of bricks kept, and replaces the 32-pixel XPM of the desktop with a scalable icon.

  • branch develop at efb2cf70

What this covers: your question of today, “Are you able to redesign the icons?”, and then “Yes, start”. It covers the icons of the ten kinds of brick and of the event, the logo, and the icon of the desktop. The icons that come from the theme (the buttons, the menus, the Images tab) stay as they are.

§1At a glance

Styles
Three candidates, all drawn in full: A flat objects in colour, B white glyphs on coloured tiles, C one-colour glyphs like GTK’s own icons (§4, I1). I recommend A.
Subjects
Mostly what they show today, redrawn. The capture becomes an Ethernet port, so it no longer looks like the tap, and the switch wrapper becomes a switch with its control folder (§5, I2).
Colour
One colour per family of bricks. A stopped brick turns grey, so a running brick has to have clear colour of its own; today’s tunnels are grey even when they run (§3).
Logo
The same V of bricks, redrawn as a vector; installed for the desktop as a scalable icon in place of the XPM (§6, I3, I5).
Files
One SVG for each icon, loaded at the size it is drawn: sharp at every zoom of the Topology tab, where today’s 96-pixel PNGs blur above 150% (§7, I4).
Plan
Three steps: the brick icons, then the logo and the desktop icon, then the guide’s pictures (§9).

§2Today

  • Each kind of brick, and the event, has a PNG of 96×96 pixels in virtualbricks/gui/data/, stored with Git LFS. They came with the resources refactoring of 2013 (b9667097) and have not changed since.
  • The windows read them at the size they show them. The Messages window uses 16 pixels; New Brick and the icon of a dragged row use 24; the rows of the Bricks and Events tabs use 32; an empty tab uses 64. The Topology tab reads each PNG at 96 pixels and lets cairo scale it to 64 points times the zoom, from 10% to 400%. Above 150% the icons are enlarged from 96 pixels and blur; at 400% one is 256 pixels.
  • A stopped brick is drawn grey in the lists, and grey and half transparent in the picture of the lab. Grey is the only sign there apart from the name.
  • The logo, virtualbricks.png, is 130×140 pixels. The About window, the main window and the tray icon use it. The desktop gets share/virtualbricks.xpm, which is 32×32, installed in share/pixmaps; virtualbricks.desktop names it as Icon=virtualbricks.
  • A virtual machine can show an image of its own in place of its icon, through the icon key of the project. That stays.

What the sheet shows:

  • Four styles side by side. Clip art in 3D (tap, capture, wire, netemu, the box of the switch wrapper), slanted boxes (switch, router), a flat screen (the virtual machine), pictures on a box (the two tunnels), and a clock (the event).
  • Uneven sizes. The virtual machine uses about a third of its square, so in a list it is 12 pixels wide next to a 32-pixel router.
  • The tap and the capture are the same green card; only the net behind the capture tells them apart, and at 16 and 24 pixels it doesn’t.
  • The tunnels are pictures on a light grey box. The box shows on the dark ground, and with so little colour a running tunnel looks almost as grey as a stopped one: tun0 runs in the pictures below, tun1 doesn’t.

Every picture of the windows on this page is a picture of the real Virtualbricks of develop, on Broadway, taken by a scenario in a scratch worktree with the icons of each style in place of the PNGs. The lab has every kind of brick; sw1, sw2, ne1, vm1 and tun0 run. The Topology tab is in the light theme, the Bricks tab in Adwaita dark.

§3What an icon has to do

  • Read from 16 to 256 pixels. Every icon is drawn on a grid of 64 units, with lines and gaps in multiples of 4: one pixel at 16, two at 32. Nothing important is smaller than 4 units.
  • Work on both themes with one file. Adwaita and Adwaita dark, the list and the picture. A large dark shape vanishes on the dark ground and a large white one on the light ground, so each icon keeps a mid-tone body (A, B). C has no body; its ink comes from the theme (§7).
  • Look stopped only when stopped. The application greys a stopped brick. An icon with little colour of its own, like today’s tunnels, looks stopped while it runs, so each icon has a clear colour (A, B), or, in C, the full colour of the text against a faded grey.
  • Differ by shape, not only colour. At 24 pixels each kind has a silhouette of its own, so it can still be told apart when grey, and by colour-blind users.
  • Show the same thing in every style, so the choice of style and the choice of subjects are separate decisions (§5).

§4Three styles

Each style has its sheet, read by GdkPixbuf as the application reads it, and the same two windows as in §2. The details are finished enough to choose from; once a style is chosen, its icons get another pass at 16 and 24 pixels.

A · Flat objects in colour

The objects seen from the front, flat, with a darker lip below for depth, as in GNOME’s application icons, in the colours of the GNOME palette. Each family has a colour: blue for the network devices (switch, switch wrapper, router), purple for the virtual machine, green for the host’s interfaces (tap, capture), orange for the cables (wire, netemu), teal for the tunnels, and yellow for the event.

  • For: it keeps what today’s icons are, devices in colour, in one style. Running and stopped are the furthest apart of the three styles, on both themes. One file serves both themes, and the code changes only in how the files are read.
  • Against: it has the most detail; at 16 pixels the ports and contacts blur into texture, though the silhouettes hold. It is also the most work to draw, and to redraw later.

B · Tiles

The glyphs of C in white on a rounded square in the family colour, as in many launchers.

  • For: every icon has the same size and weight, so the list is even, and the families stand out. Running and stopped are as far apart as in A.
  • Against: it puts a coloured square on every row of the Bricks tab, much more colour than today. On 9 October you had the colour taken off the start and stop button of each row as “too much color”. The glyph is 70% of the icon, so it is small at 16 pixels, and the lab looks like a grid of apps rather than of devices.

C · Symbolic

One-colour glyphs, like the symbolic icons of GTK on the buttons and menus, in the colour of the theme’s text: dark on the light theme, light on the dark one.

  • For: it is the quietest, and it matches the buttons of the windows. The glyphs are the simplest to draw and the sharpest at 16 pixels.
  • Against: a running brick is the colour of the text and a stopped one a faded grey of it, the weakest difference of the three. The icons need the theme’s colour when they are read, which means new code for every list, the picture, and Export as Image (§7). A virtual machine’s own image, in its icon key, keeps its colours and stands out among them.

I recommend A (I1). It keeps the character of the icons people know, gives the strongest running/stopped difference in the picture of the lab, and works on both themes with one file and no new drawing code.

§6The logo

The logo is a V of bricks: a column on the left, a staircase up to the right, and one more brick on its way to the top, over a bit of mortar. It is a good idea drawn at too few pixels. I propose to redraw it as it is, as an SVG on the same grid: a brick is 36 by 16 units, and each row of the right arm is a quarter of a brick further right. Each brick gets the lip of style A, and the mortar is left out below 48 pixels (I3).

  • The About window shows it at 128 pixels; the main window and the tray icon get it at whatever size the desktop asks for.
  • The desktop gets share/icons/hicolor/scalable/apps/virtualbricks.svg, installed by the wheel, so the menus and docks of the desktop draw it sharp at any size. virtualbricks.desktop keeps Icon=virtualbricks, and the XPM goes (I5).
  • The logo isn’t a brick, so it is the same whichever style the bricks take.

§7Files and loading

As I4 A proposes:

  • SVG files. virtualbricks/gui/data/ has switch.svg and the rest in place of the PNGs, and virtualbricks.svg for the logo. They are text, so their diffs can be read, and they leave Git LFS. The package data of pyproject.toml takes data/*.svg.
  • Read at the size drawn. The lists, New Brick and the Messages window already read at a size, so for them only the file names change. The picture of the lab reads each icon at 64 points times the zoom and keeps one copy per size, so it is sharp at 400% and a change of zoom reads each icon once.
  • One name for the extension. Seven places name .png today: brick_icon_file(), the empty Bricks and Events tabs, New Brick, the Messages window, and the logo in the main window and in About. They all go through graphics.
  • The SVG loader of GdkPixbuf. GdkPixbuf reads SVG through librsvg’s loader, librsvg2-common on Debian and Ubuntu, which GTK 3 recommends. The windows need it already: on Debian 13, adwaita-icon-theme 48 has its symbolic icons only as SVG, and Virtualbricks uses pan-down-symbolic, object-select-symbolic and others. Step 1 checks that the other targets are the same, in their containers, and the README names the package.
  • Export as Image draws the icons the picture draws, at the export’s own size, so a PNG export is sharp too. SVG and PDF exports still embed the icons as images, as today (§11).
  • Only with C: the icons need the colour of the theme’s text. The SVG text gets that colour and is read through a GdkPixbuf.PixbufLoader, one copy per colour. The picture uses the ink of its palette, and Export as Image the same.

Option B of I4 keeps PNGs: the SVGs as sources, and from them a PNG of 256 pixels for each icon in Git LFS, read as today. No loader is needed, but every icon is kept twice and the PNGs have to be made again after each change.

§8Tests and pictures

  • Unit tests (test_graphics.py, amended for .svg): every kind of brick and the event has its icon; new, each one loads at 16, 24, 32, 64 and 256 pixels; the logo loads; the picture keeps one copy of an icon per size and reads it again for a new zoom.
  • In the windows, on Broadway, light and dark, as for the pictures of this page: the lists, New Brick, the Messages window, About, and the picture at 100% and 400%.
  • Installed: the wheel has share/icons/hicolor/scalable/apps/virtualbricks.svg and no XPM.
  • The guide’s pictures that show bricks are taken again with pytest --pictures -m pictures, which rewrites only those that changed. The posts on the website keep the pictures they were published with (I6).
  • Each new test fails when the code it guards is broken, checked one break at a time.
