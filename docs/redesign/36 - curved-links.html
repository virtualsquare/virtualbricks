Curved links in a lab laid out by hand

In a lab laid out by hand, the links of the Topology tab are straight lines, and a straight line goes behind any brick in its way. This page lets you choose Graphviz’s curves instead: Graphviz routes each link around the bricks and moves none of them. The choice is kept in the project.

§1At a glance

Choice
Curved Links in the menu of the bar, under the layouts. It applies to a lab laid out by hand; Graphviz’s own layouts have curves already (§4, L1).
Curves
Graphviz routes every link around the boxes of the bricks, which stay where they are. The curves are smooth, like those of Left to Right (§5, L4).
Kept
In the settings of the project: curved_links in the [settings] of project.toml. Straight stays the default (§6, L2, L3, L6).
Too close
Graphviz can’t route between two boxes that touch. Those two bricks are routed around their icons only, and the other bricks keep their curves (§5, L5).
When
After a change that moves, adds or removes a brick, a link or a name, but not when a brick starts or stops. On this machine that takes 13 ms for 20 bricks and 0.3 s for 100 (§3).
Tests
Unit tests, a real mouse on Xvfb, and one scenario that chooses Curved Links and finds it in project.toml (§7, L7).
Plan
Three steps: the setting, the curves, the menu (§8).

§2Today

  • In a lab laid out by hand, each link is a straight line from the edge of one box to the edge of the other, aimed at their middles (page 35, M5 A). A box is the brick’s icon with its name below it.
  • A straight line goes behind any brick in its way, and can run along other links. On board S, sw1’s link to vm3 passes through w1 and sw2 and over their links, so vm3 seems to be plugged into sw2.
  • When Graphviz lays the lab out, Left to Right or Top to Bottom, dot places the bricks and routes their links around them, as curves.
  • pygraphviz 1.7 (Ubuntu 24.04) and 1.14 (the other targets) run Graphviz’s programs, dot today, and read their answer. pygraphviz 2.0, the wheel from PyPI, runs Graphviz inside the process. On this machine a layout by dot takes 12 to 18 ms whatever the size of the lab, and starting the program takes most of that.
  • The [settings] of project.toml hold the three settings of a project, each with a comment. The Settings window shows them on its project page, under Programs and Links. The console’s setting command shows and changes them. The windows of --connect get them in SettingsChanged and change them through the other Virtualbricks; settings.changed tells the windows here when one changes.
  • When a key is missing from the [settings] of a project, opening the project reports it: “missing, using the default”.

§3What Graphviz does

Checked today with Graphviz 2.42.4, this machine’s programs, through pygraphviz 1.7 and 1.14 (the environments of tox.ini for Ubuntu 24.04 and Debian 13), and with Graphviz 14.1.5 inside the pygraphviz 2.0 wheel. All three gave the same routes.

  • Pinned bricks. neato with every brick pinned where it is (pos="x,y!", in points with inputscale=72) and splines=true routes each link around the boxes of the other bricks, and moves none of them. It shifts the whole drawing by one offset, which one brick gives back.
  • Not neato -n2. Page 35 suggested neato -n2. That works only through the program. pygraphviz 2.0 lays out inside the process and takes only the program (-K) and attributes (-G, -N, -E) from its arguments: it would ignore -n2 and lay the lab out anew.
  • Other kinds of lines. Graphviz can also draw polylines, straight pieces that bend around the boxes, and right angles (splines=ortho); boards P and O in §5.
  • Boxes that touch. Graphviz keeps a margin of 3.2 points around each box, so two boxes closer than 6.4 points touch; 7.4 with Graphviz 14, which counts the outline of a box too, half a point on each side. Then it writes “Warning: some nodes with margin (3.20,3.20) touch - falling back to straight line edges” on standard error, and every link of the lab is straight (board T). Page 35 lets boxes come that close, and even overlap: a box is 84 points high, so two bricks lined up in a column 90 points apart already touch.
  • Time. Routing grows faster than the lab. The labs timed had a switch for every four bricks and about one link per brick; the best of three runs:

§4The choice

As L1 A decides, the menu of the bar gets a check item, Curved Links, in a section of its own between the layouts and the export. It is on or off for the project, and it applies while the lab is laid out by hand. While Graphviz lays the lab out, the item is greyed out: dot routes its own links as curves.

As L6 A decides, straight stays the default: a project keeps the look it has until you choose. A new project starts with a copy of the open project’s settings, so once you choose curves, the projects you create from it have them too.

As L3 A decides, the Settings window doesn’t show it: the menu of the picture is where the picture changes, and the Settings window’s Links are about what may plug into what. The console’s setting command shows and changes it, as it does every setting.

§5The curves

As L4 A decides: Graphviz’s smooth curves, like those of a lab laid out by dot. A link goes from the edge of one box to the edge of the other, as a straight link does, and bends only to go around a box in its way. A link with nothing in its way stays straight.

When they are routed

  • The tab routes the links again after a change that moves a box or a link: a move, a brick added or removed, a rename (a name can widen a box), a connection added or removed. A move can bend links far from the brick moved.
  • A brick that starts or stops changes none of that. The tab keeps the routes it has while the boxes and the links stay the same, so it doesn’t call Graphviz then, and Start All doesn’t route the lab once per brick.
  • During a drag nothing moves, so the curves stay as they are. The line to the copy of the brick stays straight and dashed, as on page 35; after the release, the links are routed again.
  • The zoom changes nothing: the routes are in points, as the boxes are.
  • Export as Image draws the curves the tab shows.

Bricks too close

As L5 B decides: when two boxes touch, Graphviz routes around the icons of those two bricks only, not around their names. The other bricks keep their whole boxes. A link may then cross one of those two names, and links to those two bricks end at the edge of their icons. Only when two icons touch, which happens when two bricks nearly cover each other, is every link straight. The tab finds the boxes that touch before it calls Graphviz, so Graphviz never has to fall back, and never writes its warning.

§6The choice in the project

As L2 A decides, the choice is a setting of the project, after the three it has:

  • An archive carries it, since it carries project.toml; Export Project and Import keep the look of the picture with the places.
  • A project.toml written before this step has no curved_links. Opening it reports the key, as any setting missing: “missing, using the default false”. The next save writes it.
  • A 2.1 project is migrated with straight links: its picture was laid out by Graphviz anyway.
  • The windows of --connect need nothing new. The setting comes in SettingsChanged with the others, and Curved Links changes it through the other Virtualbricks, as the Settings window does. Both run the same version (page 19, R11), and each routes its own picture.
  • The tab follows the setting wherever it changes: from its menu, the console, or another window of --connect.

§7Tests

  • The setting (test_settings.py, test_projectfile.py): curved_links false by default, written with its comment and read; a new project copies it; the console’s setting shows and sets it.
  • The curves (test_topology.py): no brick moved; a link with a box in its way goes around it, and one with nothing in its way stays straight; the drawing’s offset given back; two boxes that touch routed around their icons; two icons that touch giving straight links; no warning on standard error; a lab without links not sent to Graphviz.
  • The tab (test_topology.py of the windows): Curved Links on and off, greyed out while Graphviz lays the lab out; the setting changed by the menu, and the picture following a change made elsewhere; a brick started without a call to Graphviz.

  • Each new test fails when the code it guards is broken, checked one break at a time. After step 3, a real mouse on Xvfb in the whole application.
