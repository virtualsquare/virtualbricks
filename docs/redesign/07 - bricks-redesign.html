A Clearer Bricks Tab

Each brick becomes a row that says what it is, whether it runs and what it is plugged into, with Start or Stop and its menu at hand. A search finds a brick, and configuring one keeps the window’s menus and tabs.

  • branch mg/abandon-glade at 19bc793
  • revision 7 · 29 September 2026
  • implemented

What changed: revision 2 applies your answers: the recommended one for ten questions, and the Running tab merged into this one (B10 B). Board X5 becomes A7, with the process menu of a running brick. The menu (§5), the plan (§7) and the tests (§8) take the Running tab in. The boards of the options not chosen stay for reference, X1 to X4.

Implemented in six commits from 66095a1, one per step of §7, with these differences. The keys show only in the menu of the right button: GTK 3 draws none in a popover, the menu of a row’s button. That popover is made each time it opens, so Connect To is always current. Under the settings of a brick, Cancel and OK are the tab’s, always in sight under the scrolled panel, and call the panel’s own. Start All starts only the bricks that can start, and Stop All stops only the running ones. On the way: a switch’s rename told nobody, removing a brick unplugs the others without telling them, and Restart sent an ACPI reset to a virtual machine; the first and the last are mended, and the list covers the second.

Since then: the modules of §7 are a package of their own, gui/mainwindow/bricks/, with the tab in tab.py (3cbac0e); the rows, the row above the list and the settings page are in gui/mainwindow/rowtab.py, which the Events and Images tabs share (e48fc4b). The Events tab took the same shape, first item of §10, and put When It Starts and When It Stops in the menu of a brick, in place of Attach Event… (step 8). The panels are in gui/mainwindow/bricks/config/ (678ef50), and the Drives tab of a virtual machine lists its disks, each with an image picker (step 9). The settings have new names, which the summaries read, and Resume passes the saved state to the start (step 10). The panels themselves, an item of §10, were redesigned in step 11: each works on a draft of its brick, OK waits while the draft has an error, and the virtual machine’s has a sidebar of sections; the router still has no panel. New Brick, whose window this step kept and whose title and label §10 asked for, became a popover of the kinds in step 12: a click makes a brick named after its kind, selects its row and shows its settings (9d35fb0). The page of an empty project of A5 no longer offers New Brick itself: the button in the row above the list does (292f41f).

§1At a glance

Rows
One row per brick: its icon, grey when stopped; its name; its kind and a short summary; its state; then Start or Stop and the menu (B1, B4, B8).
Controls
A row above the list: New Brick, the search, the count of running bricks, and Start All and Stop All (B3, B7).
Menu
The same menu from the row’s button, the right button and the Topology tab, translated, with its keys, and Connect To (B6, B9).
Settings
Configure shows the brick’s panel in the tab, in place of the list, under a line that names the brick. The menus and the other tabs stay (B2). A double click or Enter configures (B5).
Running
The Running tab goes. A switch above the list shows only the bricks that run, with their process, and the menu of a running brick has today’s process actions (B10).
Unchanged
The panels themselves, the New Brick window and the order of the bricks (B11).

§2The tab today

  • The toolbar has GTK 2 stock icons, a green and a red ball among them, with labels under them. It takes 60 pixels of height.
  • The list has five columns: an icon, Status, Type, Name and Parameters. The name comes fourth. The headings don’t sort, and the Icon column has no use for a heading.
  • The kinds are the names in the code: “Qemu” for a virtual machine, “TunnelListen” for a tunnel server.
  • Parameters is the text the console prints. A virtual machine shows the path of its QEMU. A wire says “Not yet configured. Left plug is disconnected and right plug is disconnected”.
  • A stopped brick’s icon is grey and hatched: saturate_and_pixelate is called with pixelate on. The Topology picture turns it off.
  • The rows are 50 pixels high for 48-pixel icons, so the tab shows seven bricks.
  • A double click starts or stops a brick. On a running virtual machine it stops it at once, without asking.
  • Everything else is in the right button’s menu, which nothing points to. Its labels aren’t translated: “_Start/Stop”, “Make a C_opy”.
  • Configure hides the tabs, and the window becomes the panel until OK or Cancel (T1). There is no panel for a router, so configuring one fails.
  • Dropping a brick on another connects them, and nothing says so.
  • A project without bricks shows an empty white list.
  • The Running tab shows the list of this tab through a filter, so the two share a model. It checks what runs every two seconds.
  • In the Running tab’s menu, Stop pauses the process, with SIGSTOP; it isn’t the Stop of the Bricks tab. On a virtual machine, Restart sends an ACPI reset, as Send ACPI hard reset does: the virtual machine’s menu has its own method with the name that Restart calls. And Send ACPI powerdown is what Stop already does to a virtual machine.

§6Configuring a brick

With B2 A, the tab holds a GtkStack of two pages: the list, and the settings of one brick (A3).

  • The settings page has a line with the brick’s icon, its name and “Switch settings”, then the panel as today, with its Cancel and OK. The panels don’t change: they still call the window’s curtain_down(), which now goes back to the list.
  • Configure from the Topology tab’s menu shows the Bricks tab on the brick’s settings. An event still takes the whole window until the Events tab gets the same page (§10).
  • The other tabs stay usable. Coming back to the Bricks tab shows the settings where they were.
  • The settings close, as with Cancel, when their brick is deleted, and when another project opens.
  • Configuring a second brick while one is open closes the first, as with Cancel. The window’s title keeps the project’s name.
  • The settings of a virtual machine are the biggest, and the main window’s tabs and the brick’s line take about 80 pixels more than today. The panel sits in a scrolled window, so it never makes the main window taller; step 5 checks it at the default size, 850 by 600, on Broadway.

§7Implementation plan

With the answers of §9. Each step is a commit with its tests, stopping for review after each. The new code goes in virtualbricks/gui/mainwindow/.

  1. What a row says. brickinfo.py, without widgets: the kind of a brick in words, its summary (§4), its state (running, stopped, not connected, not configured), its process, and the drop’s rule, which moves here from the tab: which bricks a brick can connect to, and connecting them.
  2. The menu. brickmenu.py: the Gio.Menu of a brick and a group of actions that do what today’s items do, with the sensitivity of §5, and the Process submenu of a running brick with the Running tab’s actions, Restart mended. The Topology tab uses it; BrickPopupMenu and VMPopupMenu leave gui.py.
  3. The rows. bricklist.py: a GtkListBox with a row per brick, kept in step with the factory: added, removed, changed. Each row has its icon, its text, its state, its button and the menu’s button. The list filters by a search text and by running, says when nothing matches, and connects a brick dropped on another.
  4. The tab. BricksTab puts the row of controls over the list: New Brick, the search, the All and Running switch, the count, Start All and Stop All; the page of an empty project; the keys. The Running tab goes, with running.py, JobMenu, VMJobMenu and IJobMenu.
  5. The settings in the tab. The stack of the list and the settings page. The window’s curtain_up() sends a brick to the tab and keeps the whole-window panel for events; curtain_down() goes back to the list. A double click and Enter configure, in this tab and in the Topology tab.
  6. The rest. What nothing uses any more: BricksBindingList, and the cell renderers of widgets.py if no list uses them. A check of the whole application on Broadway.

§8Tests

  • What a row says: the kind and the summary of each kind of brick, connected or not, and the three states; the bricks a brick can connect to, both ways.
  • The menu: its items for a brick, a running brick, a virtual machine and a router, what each action calls, Connect To, and the Process submenu, Restart of a virtual machine among them.
  • The rows: added, removed and changed with the factory; the icon, the state and the button of each of the four states; the search by name and kind, the Running switch, and the text when nothing matches; the drop.
  • The tab: the buttons and what they do, the count, the empty project, the keys; Start All leaving the bricks that aren’t connected.
  • The settings: shown from the tab and from the Topology tab, closed by OK, Cancel, a deletion and another project.

As before, each new test is checked against the code it guards, broken on a copy.

Why these: rows say more than columns in the same width, and they hold a button per brick; a table only wins with many more bricks than a lab usually has (B1). The settings in the tab keep one place for everything, change no panel, and never hide the tabs (B2). A floating bar suits a picture or a text, but over a list it covers the buttons of a row (B3). A double click that stops a running virtual machine is too easy to make by mistake, and a visible button makes it unnecessary (B4, B5). Your answer to B10 puts each process next to its brick, one list instead of two. The order of the bricks can change later, on its own (B11).
