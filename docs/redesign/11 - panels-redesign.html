Virtualbricks Brick Panels Redesign

New settings panels for the bricks, moved one at a time. Each row uses the schema’s own words, a state object sits between the panel and the brick, and the virtual machine’s panel knows which QEMU the machine will run. widgets.py and the sensitivity classes from the Glade era go with the old panels.

  • branch mg/abandon-glade at 83fb7f4
  • revision 2 · 28 September 2026
  • every decision made

What this covers: your request of today: a redesign of the brick configuration panels, with mock-ups and options, and a plan that moves one panel at a time. gui/widgets.py is deprecated as far as it can be, and here that means all of it. The panels produce a state object that then configures the brick, and never work on the brick’s configuration directly. The virtual machine’s panel serves every QEMU from 6.2 on, each with its own options. This is the TODO item “Redesign the configuration panels” (07 §10, 10 §13), and it takes up three more: readable boot devices, the icon chooser that raises, and the unused networkcards.py.

What changed: revision 2 records your answer, every decision as recommended, P1 to P11, and says in §4 why the labels and the help are marked with N_(). The work happens on the branch mg/brick-panels, a commit per step of §8, and the branch is merged back into mg/abandon-glade at the end. The suggestions of §11 are in the TODO.

§1At a glance

What
The settings of a brick, where the Bricks tab shows them: new panels for the nine kinds of bricks that have one, each moved on its own, and a state object between the panel and the brick. The router still has none.
State object
A draft of the brick’s settings, made when they open. It is a copy of the configuration, checked by the schema as you type, with the connections by name. OK applies the draft to the brick in one go. Cancel drops it, and the brick never saw a change (P1).
Rows
A row per setting, grouped in sections: a label and a line of help from the schema, a standard GTK widget chosen by the field’s kind, and a picker with a search for QEMU’s long lists (P3).
Virtual machine
Eight sections in a sidebar. The panel asks the machine’s QEMU what it has, as the start does. The lists are that QEMU’s. A setting it lacks keeps its value and shows the warning that the start writes to the logs (P7).
What goes
gui/widgets.py as a whole; the sensitivity classes of config/base.py and their four interfaces; _PlugMixin; the zope adapter that finds a panel; the network card dialogs and their menu; and the unused networkcards.py.
Plan
Eight steps, a commit each, stopping after each one for your review. The groundwork comes with the switch, then the small panels, then the tap and the wire, then Netemu. The virtual machine takes three steps, and its new panel is built beside the old one. Cleanup comes last.
Decisions
Eleven questions, P1–P11, all decided as recommended on 28 September. The work happens on the branch mg/brick-panels.

§2The panels today

What gets in the way

  • Cancel doesn’t cancel everything. The Add Network Card dialog adds the card to the machine when it closes, and removing a card removes it at once, so Cancel keeps both. Netemu’s OK writes its states into the brick’s Markov manager before it calls set().
  • Settings half applied. brick.set() assigns one field after the other, and the kind of each field checks its value. A Tap given the netmask 255.255.0.300 gets address_mode manual and its new address before set() raises on the netmask. The error goes to the log, and the settings close as if they were saved.
  • Nothing is checked before OK. OK takes a custom kernel with no kernel, a CD-ROM image with no file, or a machine type that the QEMU doesn’t have. The machine then starts without the first two, silently, and the start warns about the third.
  • The virtual machine’s panel knows its QEMU only for the machine types and CPU models. The sound cards (5), the card models (9, in the Add Network Card dialog) and the boot devices (3) are fixed lists. SDL and KVM are offered whatever the QEMU has. Since the settings redesign, the start leaves out what the QEMU lacks and writes a warning in File › Logs. The panel doesn’t mention it.
  • The words don’t match the settings. The panel says “Use VGA instead of Default”, “disable graphical output” and “Used RAM: MB”. The schema says “A standard VGA card instead of the machine’s”, “No display at all” and “Memory in MiB”. acpi has no widget, and choosing an icon raises NotImplementedError.
  • Heavy machinery for a check box. Greying a spin button while its check box is off takes StateManager, State, CompoundPrerequisite and three *Control classes, with four zope interfaces. The List, ComboBox, ListEntry and CellRendererFormattable of widgets.py stand in for a combo box with ids. Both came from the Glade files.
  • Hard to test. The logic lives in signal handlers. Only the disks, the image picker and the program list of the virtual machine have tests, 676 lines.

§3The state object

The panels stop working on the brick. When a brick’s settings open, its panel gets a draft, the state of the settings as the panel shows them. Every change goes into the draft. The brick sees nothing until OK, and Cancel drops the draft. In the code it is called a draft because State already names two other things: the running state of a brick in brickinfo.py, and the sensitivity class that goes.

What a draft holds

  • Settings. A copy of the brick’s configuration record, the same schema class (attr.evolve(brick.config)). The field’s kind checks every assignment, as in the brick. A value the kind refuses is kept as typed, with the reason, and the copy keeps its last good value.
  • Links. The socket each plug joins, by name, or none. A virtual machine’s cards are values: their model, their MAC, what they join, and which card of the machine each one was, if any.
  • Extras. What a brick has beyond its record: a virtual machine’s disks and USB devices, a Netemu’s states and transitions.
  • Context. What the panel shows but doesn’t change: the sockets a plug can join (with allow_female_plugs), the ports in use, the host’s interfaces, the answers of the QEMU. It is given to the draft, so a test can give its own.

Problems

A draft has two kinds of problems. The panel shows each one under its row.

  • Errors block OK: a value the kind refuses, like an address that isn’t IPv4, or two settings that don’t go together, like a custom kernel with no kernel, a CD-ROM image with no file, or fewer ports than the bricks plugged into the switch. OK stays insensitive, and the footer names the first error.
  • Lacks don’t block anything. The installed program doesn’t have a setting: the brick will start without it, and the panel shows the same words that the start writes to the logs (§6).

Applying a draft

  1. The settings that differ, in one brick.set(). A running brick takes them through its cbset_ methods, as today.
  2. The links. A plug joins its new socket, or none. A virtual machine’s cards are kept, changed, removed or added, in their order.
  3. The extras: the images and modes of the disks, the USB devices, Netemu’s states and transitions.
  4. One “changed” for the brick, so the Bricks tab and the Topology draw it once.

An image added from a disk’s picker is the exception, as it is today. It joins the project’s library at once, because the library isn’t the brick’s. The disk only points at the image after OK.

A running brick

An info bar at the top of the settings says what OK changes at once. It reads “sw1 is running. Ports, hub mode and fast spanning tree change at once; the rest when it starts again”, or “vm1 is running. The settings take effect when it starts again”. The words come from the brick’s cbset_ methods, until a declared list replaces them (P10, and the TODO).

§4Rows from the schema

A panel is a list of rows grouped in titled sections, as in GNOME Settings. Each row is bound to one field of the draft, by name. Its label and its line of help come from the schema, its widget from the field’s kind, and its range too. A row writes to the draft when it changes, and after every change the panel asks the draft which rows are in use and what’s wrong.

Labels and help

FieldInfo already has a label, and no field sets it. Each field gets a short label, and the help it has becomes the line under the label. Both are marked with N_(), which returns its text unchanged. The files’ comments stay in English (C3 of the settings redesign), so the schema must keep the English text: _() in the class body would translate it as the module is imported, and the files would get comments in the desktop’s language. A plain string, though, is never extracted by xgettext, and nothing would translate it. The marker is the standard gettext way out: the translation hook extracts the marked strings with --keyword=N_, and the panel translates them with _() when it shows them. So a setting is described in one place, and the panel says what the file says (P4).

Rows in use

A draft knows which settings go with others. vnc_display goes with use_vnc, the Tap’s addresses with address_mode manual, and Netemu’s values from right to left with their _symmetric switch off. draft.uses(name) answers from a table in the draft’s class, following it along: initrd needs use_initrd, which needs use_kernel. The panel greys the rows that aren’t used, and a greyed row keeps its value, as the files do. The rule is tested without GTK (P9).

What the panel does

  • It asks the program the machine names. The programs are the QEMUs in the project’s qemu_path, as today. The chosen one is asked through programs.qemu(path), once a session, the same answers the start uses. Until they come, a picker shows only the machine’s choice, and a switch its value.
  • The lists are the program’s. Machine types and CPU models, with the default first. Card models are the devices in “Network devices”, and sound cards are the devices in “Sound devices” that work on their own, plus the PC speaker. Boot devices get readable names: the default, the first disk, the CD-ROM, the floppy, the network.
  • Nothing is hidden, and nothing changes by itself. A value the program lacks stays chosen. Its picker lists it first as the machine’s choice, and its row shows the lack. A switch the program can’t honour, like SDL without qemu-system-gui or KVM without kvm, stays as it was, with its note (P7).
  • The sidebar marks a section with a lack, so a machine moved to another distribution shows at once where to look (board D).
  • A program that isn’t installed puts an info bar over the sections, for example “qemu-system-aarch64 isn’t in /usr/bin: the lists show only vm1’s choices”. Every setting stays editable.

One source for the warnings

The checks live in VirtualMachine.command() today, mixed with the options they leave out. They move into one function that returns, for each setting the program lacks, its key and its words. command() leaves those settings out and writes the warnings with the machine’s name and the key, as it does now, and the panel shows the same words under the row. A test keeps the two in step on the seven recordings.

The machine’s properties, which decide whether ACPI goes off with -machine acpi=off or with -no-acpi, are asked for the chosen machine type while ACPI is off, as the start asks them.

§7The code

New

virtualbricks/bricks/draft.py

Draft: the copy of the settings, the links by name, set(), uses(), problems() and changes(). Problem, with its key, its words and whether it blocks OK. apply(draft). No GTK, like the rest of bricks/ (P2).

a draft class beside each brick that needs one

In the brick’s own module: SwitchDraft with its lower bound on the ports, TapDraft with its addresses, NetemuDraft with the states and transitions, and VirtualMachineDraft with the cards, disks and USB devices and the WITH table. The simpler bricks use Draft itself.

Form builds the sections and rows of §4 on a draft: switch(), spin(), choice(), entry(), file(), picker(), socket(). Picker is the popover with a search, on the pattern of the image picker. Each row shows its problems and greys itself from draft.uses().

gui/mainwindow/bricks/config/vm/

The virtual machine’s panel, a module per section. disks.py and imagepicker.py move in, and the disks write to the draft instead of the machine.

§8Implementation plan

A commit per step. At every step the application works, the panels not yet moved work as today, and each step ends with the tests, ruff and black, a look at the panel on Broadway, and a stop for your review.

  1. Groundwork, with the switch.

    bricks/draft.py; form.py with the switch, spin, entry and choice rows; panel.py; the settings page for a new panel, with OK following the problems and the info bar for a running brick; labels and N_() in SwitchConfig. SwitchPanel takes the place of SwitchConfigController, and the Bricks tab looks in PANELS before the adapter.

    Tests: the draft (a copy, set, problems, uses, changes, apply, a running switch), the rows, the switch’s panel.

  2. The switch wrapper, the capture and the two tunnels.

    The file row, the host’s interfaces as context, and the socket picker, whose first users these are.

  3. The tap and the wire.

    TapDraft checks the addresses as you type. The wire gets two ends.

  4. Netemu.

    NetemuDraft with its states and transitions; the list of states, the values with Both ways, the grid of transitions. Netemu stops using widgets.py, StateManager and the help buttons, and _PlugMixin goes with it.

  5. The virtual machine, 1: the draft.

    VirtualMachineDraft (settings, cards, disks, USB devices, the WITH table) and its apply; lacks() out of command(), shared. No change on screen.

    Tests: the draft; lacks() on the seven recordings, and that command() leaves out exactly what it lists.

  6. The virtual machine, 2: the new panel, beside the old one.

    The sidebar; the Picker; the Machine, Disks, CD-ROM and boot, Display, and Sound and USB sections. The Bricks tab still opens the old panel, and the new one is reached by its tests.

  7. The virtual machine, 3: the switch.

    The Network, Kernel and Advanced sections; the Bricks tab opens the new panel. qemuconfig.py, networkcards.py, the card dialogs and their menu, widgets.py, and the sensitivity classes with their interfaces go.

  8. Cleanup.

    PANELS in place of IConfigController and config_panel_factory; the unused interfaces; the README’s source layout; the TODO; this page’s “Implemented”.

§9Tests

  • The drafts, without GTK, in tests/bricks/. Making a draft leaves the brick alone. A value the kind refuses becomes a problem and keeps the copy’s last good value. uses() follows the WITH table along. changes() lists only what differs, and apply() calls brick.set() once with it. The links move, a running brick gets its cbset_ calls, and the brick says “changed” once. The virtual machine’s cards are kept, changed, removed and added in order.
  • lacks() on the seven recordings. pc-i440fx-jammy lacks on 10.0.13, virtio-sound-pci on 6.2.0, and every card on a switch is unplugged on the Ubuntu QEMUs. ACPI goes off with -machine acpi=off where the machine type has the property, else with -no-acpi where the QEMU still has it, else it lacks. And command() leaves out exactly what lacks() lists.
  • The panels, with GTK, in tests/gui/mainwindow/bricks/config/, in an OffscreenWindow, skipped without a display. There is a row per field, showing the draft’s value, and a change in a row reaches the draft. Rows grey as uses() says, and OK follows the errors. A picker filters, keeps a choice the QEMU lacks at the top, and fills when the answers come.
  • On Broadway, after each step: the panel in the real window, at 760 pixels and wider, with a long name and a long list.
