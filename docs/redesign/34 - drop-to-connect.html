Drop to connect, in the picture

In the Bricks list, a brick dropped on another connects the two when one can plug into the other. This page brings the same drop to the picture of the Topology tab: press a brick and move, and a line follows the pointer to the brick it will plug into. On the way it mends the list, whose rows never start a drag with a real mouse.

§1At a glance

Drag
Press a brick in the picture and move: a line goes from it to the pointer. Over a brick that it connects to, the line ends on that brick, which lights up, and the words under it say Connect tap1 to sw2. Release, and the two connect (§3, C1, C2).
Rules
The list’s, unchanged: a brick with a free plug, or a virtual machine, which gets a new card, plugs into the first socket of the other. A release anywhere else does nothing, and so does Escape (§4).
Edges
Zoomed in, the picture scrolls while the pointer is near one of its edges, to reach a brick out of view (§4, C4).
List
The list starts the drag, with the row under the press: a row can’t, having no window of its own. Checked today with a real mouse, before and after (§2, §5, C3).
Tests
Unit tests that send the view its events; a real mouse on Xvfb, by hand, after each step. No end-to-end scenario: AT-SPI sees no brick in the picture (§6, C5).
Plan
Two steps: the list, then the picture (§7).

§2Today

The list

Every row of the Bricks list is a source and a destination of a drag of the target virtualbricks/brick, within Virtualbricks, for a link (prepare() in bricklist.py). While it lasts, the brick’s icon, at 24 pixels, and its name follow the pointer. Over a row it can connect to, the row is framed and the name becomes Connect tap to sw. The drop calls engine.connect(): here it connects the two, and in the windows of --connect it sends Connect to the other Virtualbricks. The unit tests call the handlers of the drag themselves, and pass.

With a real mouse, no row starts a drag. Checked today on Xvfb: the Bricks list of develop in a window, with a tap and a switch; xdotool pressed on the name of the tap, moved onto the row of the switch in ten steps, and released. The press reached the list, never the row: drag-begin never came, and the tap stayed unplugged. A GtkListBoxRow has no window of its own. GTK gives a press to the widget that owns the window under the pointer, here the list, then to its parents, never down to a row, so the rows’ drag_source_set() never sees it. e2e/TODO.md says so since 3 October (9e9ff902); BUGS.md doesn’t. The guide never mentions the drop, only Connect To in the menu of a brick.

The same check with the list as the source of the drag, and the row under the press as the brick dragged, started the drag, and the tap was plugged into the switch (§5).

The picture

  • A press on the background and a move drag the picture. On a brick, the left button only takes the focus; a double click opens its settings; the right button opens its menu, the list’s, with Connect To.
  • The brick under the pointer sits on a disc of the selection colour, with its name, kind and state in a tooltip. A brick is a box: its icon, and its name below it.
  • After a brick changes, the tab lays the lab out again with Graphviz; fitted, the picture fits again.

The rules

brickinfo.connection(source, destination) decides, for the list and for the menu. When the source has a socket and the destination takes a plug, the destination plugs into the source’s first socket; else, the other way round. A brick takes a plug when one of its plugs goes nowhere, or when it is a virtual machine, which gets a new network card each time. So a tap dropped on a switch and the switch dropped on the tap make the same link. Two switches, two machines, or a tap already plugged don’t connect.

§3The drag

As C1 A and C2 A decide. The left button pressed on a brick starts nothing until the pointer has moved by GTK’s drag threshold, 8 pixels by default: before that, a press and a release are a click, and a double click still opens the settings. A press on the background still moves the picture.

Once the drag starts, the brick dragged keeps its disc, and a line in the selection colour goes from the middle of its icon. While it goes nowhere it is dashed and ends at the pointer. Over a brick that it connects to, it is solid and ends in the middle of that brick’s icon; that brick gets its disc too, and the words under it, on a dark box like the bar’s, say what a release will do. The line goes under the bricks, over the links. The pointer is the closed hand of a drag; the tooltips wait for the end.

§4The drop

  • On a brick it connects to: the tab calls engine.connect(source, target), as the list does, and a failure goes to the log. The bricks change, the tab lays the lab out again, and the new link shows. Graphviz may then move other bricks; moving them by hand, and keeping them there, is another item of IDEAS.
  • Anywhere else, or Escape, or a lost grab, as when a window opens over the main window: nothing happens, and the line goes.
  • Near an edge (C4 A): while the pointer is less than 32 pixels from an edge of the view, the view scrolls that way, every 30 milliseconds, by up to 20 pixels, more the nearer the edge.
  • Zoom: Ctrl and the wheel, and the keys, still zoom during the drag, and the line follows.
  • A change in the lab during the drag, as a brick that starts or stops, or one made from the console: the tab lays the lab out again. The drag holds the bricks, not their places, so it goes on over the new layout; it ends if the brick dragged is deleted.
  • Over a connection: connect() is the engine’s, so in the windows of --connect the other Virtualbricks connects the two, as for the list.

§5The list

As C3 A decides, the list mends its drag before the picture copies it. The list, which has a window, takes the press and remembers the row under it. Once the pointer has moved by the drag threshold with the button down, the list starts the drag itself, drag_begin_with_coordinates(), with that row’s brick. A press below the last row starts nothing, and the buttons of a row take their own presses, as today. The rows stay the destinations; the icon, the frame and the words stay as they are.

The words of both drags are the same, Connect {brick} to {other}, the brick dragged first. Dropping sw2 on tap1 says Connect sw2 to tap1 and plugs the tap into the switch, as today.

§6Tests

  • The list (test_bricklist.py, amended): the list starts the drag, with the row under the press; not before the threshold, and not from below the last row.
  • The view (test_topologyview.py, new tests): the drag starts on a brick, with the left button, past the threshold; the target and the words, the line solid or dashed, the discs; the signal of the drop, and none after a release elsewhere, Escape or a lost grab; the scroll near each edge, and its end; a new layout during the drag; the brick dragged deleted. test_picture.py: the line under the bricks.
  • The tab (test_topology.py): a drop connects through the engine.
  • Each new test fails when the code it guards is broken, checked one break at a time.
  • A real mouse: after each step, today’s check on Xvfb with xdotool, for the list and then for the picture of the whole application, with a screenshot of the drag. The script stays out of the repository.
  • End to end (C5): none, by A. AT-SPI sees the drawing area but no brick in it, and PyGObject can’t give the area a child for each brick: Atk’s ref_child is not among the methods that a Python class can override, checked today with PyGObject 3.58.1. The list’s drop can’t be played on Broadway, which has no drag and drop. e2e/TODO.md says both.

§8Decisions

QuestionOptionsDecided
C1 How the picture drags a brick

A By itself: the view follows the pointer, as it does when the background is dragged, and draws what the drag shows (§3). Its tests send it events; it works wherever the picture does, Broadway too.

B With GTK’s drag and drop, as the list: the drawing area as the source and the destination of virtualbricks/brick, the icon following the pointer. Nothing outside the picture takes a brick of the picture, so it gives nothing that A lacks; it needs the drag and drop of the display, which Broadway lacks, and GTK 4 replaces its API.

 A
C2 What the drag shows

A A line from the brick: dashed to the pointer, solid to a brick it connects to, which lights up with the words under it (boards A1, A2).

B As the list: the brick’s icon and the words follow the pointer, and the brick under it lights up when they connect (board B).

 A
C3 The list, whose rows never start a drag

A Mend it first, step 1: the list starts the drag with the row under the press (§5).

B Leave it, with an entry in BUGS.md; only the picture gets a drop.

 A
C4 A brick out of view

A The view scrolls while the pointer is near an edge (§4).

B No scroll: zoom out during the drag, with Ctrl and the wheel or Ctrl+−, to bring the brick into view.

 A

A None: the unit tests and a real mouse on Xvfb by hand; e2e/TODO.md says why (§6).

B A step that finds a brick in the picture by its pixels, in what broadwayd showed, as recording.py decodes it, at 100%; then a scenario drags tap1 onto sw1, and the row of tap1 in the Bricks list says on sw1. It ties the steps to the drawing: other icons, colours or greys break them.
