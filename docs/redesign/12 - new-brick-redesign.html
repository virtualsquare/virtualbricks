What this covers: your request of today, a redesign of the New Brick window with mock-ups and options. Page 07 left this window unchanged, and its §10 put its title and label in the TODO. The panels that open after the click are those of page 11, unchanged.

What changed: revision 2 records your answers. New Brick is the popover of revision 1’s X2 (N1 C). Each entry shows one line with its issue, and a longer tooltip describes the problem. The name is chosen automatically and is unique in the project (N2 D), so the window goes, with its name field and the TODO item about its title and label. N8, whether an entry with an issue can be clicked, came with the popover. The rest is as recommended. §3 now has the words of every entry, §4 shows the popover and a tooltip, and the window of revision 1 and the other shapes stay at its end, not chosen.

Implemented on 29 September 2026 on the branch mg/new-brick, a commit per step: 87805d8 the kinds, 9d35fb0 the popover, and one for the documents. The differences from the plan: new_brick() doesn’t check a tap’s 15 characters, since it also opens the projects, and a project with such a tap must still open; factory.check_name() has them, and Rename, the console’s new and the popover ask it. The words of the kinds are translated with _(), as KINDS already is, not marked N_(), and a Kind holds the class of its bricks, whose type the factory takes. The new() of the rows tab keeps no argument: the Bricks tab points the popover at the New Brick shown, as the Images tab does with its menu. A new machine’s program is qemu-system-i386, the default of its settings, and that is the one its issue names. The words of an issue are those of §3 and P2 now; each ends “The brick can be made now, and starts once it is installed.” GTK shows a tooltip under the pointer, and for the row with the keyboard’s focus after Ctrl+F1. The popover was checked in the whole application on Broadway: New Brick, a click on Tap, tap2 made after tap1, its settings, its row selected after Cancel, and the popover open again on Tap.

Since then: the page of an empty project has no New Brick of its own, nor New Event or Add Image in the other tabs (292f41f): the New of the row above the list is always there, and the popover opens under it only. P1 and §5 still speak of the second button.

§1At a glance

What
New Brick opens a popover of the kinds, under the button. A click makes the brick at once, with a name of its own, and opens its settings in the tab, as a new event’s open (N1, N5).
Entries
Ten, in three groups: a picture, the kind, and one line, which says what the kind is or, when it has one, its issue. A longer tooltip describes the kind, and the problem first when there is one (N3).
Name
Chosen automatically: the kind’s short form and the first free number, sw3, vm4, tap1, unique in the project among bricks, events and images. Rename (F2) changes it, and now checks a tap’s 15 characters (N2).
Issues
A program that this computer lacks, with the package that has it. The entry can still be clicked: the brick is made, and starts once the program is installed (N8). The router is listed too, and says it has no settings yet (N4).
After the click
The new row is selected and its settings open, the plugs in nothing: the panel’s warning says what the brick lacks (N6).
What goes
The window, gui/dialogs/newbrick.py: its ten buttons that the keyboard can’t reach, its name field and its error in a tooltip. With it goes the TODO item “Give the New Brick window a title, and its name field a label” (07 §10).
Plan
Three steps, a commit each, with a stop for your review after each: the kinds without GTK, then the popover, then the documents.
Decisions
Eight, N1–N8, all decided on 29 September: the popover, with a line and a tooltip per entry, and automatic names; the rest as recommended.

§2The window today

NewBrickDialog, in gui/dialogs/newbrick.py, is a converted Glade file of 471 lines. It has ten toggle buttons with pictures of 96 pixels, a name field, and Cancel and OK. BricksTab.new() opens it, from the New Brick button and from the page of an empty project. OK calls factory.new_brick(type, name) and closes the window. The new brick shows up at the end of the list. The window has no tests.

What gets in the way

  • No title, no label. The window takes the program’s name, “virtualbricks”. The name field has only a placeholder, which GTK hides while the field has the focus, that is, from the moment the window opens (TODO, 07 §10).
  • The kinds can’t be reached from the keyboard. The ten buttons don’t take the focus, so Tab goes from the name straight to Cancel and OK. Without the mouse, only a switch can be made.
  • Pictures, not words. A kind is a picture and a word or two. “Switch Wrapper”, “Capture Interface”, “Netemu” and the two tunnels mean nothing to someone new to VDE. The brick classes have a one-line summary for the project files, and the window doesn’t show it.
  • An order with no logic. Switch, wire, Netemu, tap, switch wrapper; then tunnel client, virtual machine, tunnel server, capture, router: the Glade grid, row by row.
  • Missing programs aren’t mentioned. A kind whose program isn’t installed is offered like any other: a tunnel without vde2-cryptcab, or a router, whose vde_router no distribution ships and which has no settings. The brick gets made, and its start fails with a line in File › Logs.
  • A name to type every time. Nothing is suggested, and OK stays grey until a name is typed. New Event suggests new_event.
  • The reason is a tooltip. A bad name turns the field red and OK grey (T1), and the reason shows only under the pointer. The one for a name in use isn’t translated.
  • A tap with a long name can’t start. The host’s interface takes the brick’s name (Tap.command()), and Linux allows 15 bytes for it. Nothing checks this, so vde_plug2tap fails at the start.
  • Then nothing. Seven kinds out of ten can’t start until they are plugged in or given a path or an address (§3). You have to find the new row and open its settings yourself.
  • Words not translated. “Switch”, “Wire” and seven more are plain strings; only “Tap” is marked. brickinfo.KINDS already has them all translated, in other words.

§3What a new brick needs

What each kind needs before it can start, what it runs, and the name it gets. Seven out of ten can’t start as they are made: they need a switch, a path or an address, and their panel is where they get it. That is the case for opening the settings after the click (N5).

The groups say where a brick sits in the lab. Machines and switches are what the lab is made of. Links join switches, here or on another computer. The last two join this computer to the lab. The order within a group puts the most common kind first.

The names use the short forms of the sample project, tests/sample.py, with the first number that no brick, event or image of the project has: with vm1 to vm3, a new machine is vm4, and with vm2 deleted, vm2. They fit a tap’s 15 characters and the sockets. If the project’s folder leaves no room even for them, every entry is greyed, with the reason in its tooltip: the one case where a click can’t make a brick.

When the kind has an issue, the line is the issue instead, in the colour of warnings, and the tooltip starts with a paragraph on the problem:

  • One program missing: the line says “vde_cryptcab isn’t installed”, and the tooltip “vde_cryptcab isn’t installed: the package vde2-cryptcab has it. The brick can be made now, and starts once it is installed.” The package comes from programs.PACKAGES, as in the start’s own message.
  • No package: “vde_router isn’t installed, and no distribution ships it.”
  • Several programs: the line names the first; the tooltip has a sentence for each, and ends “…starts once they are installed.” A wire without vde2 lacks dpipe and vde_plug.
  • A choice of programs: Netemu has an issue only when both are missing: “Neither vde-netemu (vde-netemu) nor wirefilter (vde2) is installed.”
  • A virtual machine: a new machine runs qemu-system-i386, the default of its settings, looked for in the QEMU folder: “qemu-system-i386 isn’t installed: the package qemu-system-x86 has it.”

Today this knowledge is spread out or missing. brickinfo.KINDS has the words. The programs are only inside each brick’s command(), as the vde_program() calls. The packages are in programs.PACKAGES. No description exists besides the summary of each class, in English, for the files. Nothing knows about a tap’s 15 characters.

§5The code

  • gui/mainwindow/bricks/newbrick.py, next to the tab that uses it. NewBrickPopover(gui) is a Gtk.Popover holding a Gtk.ListBox, with a header per group and a row per kind: a picture, the kind, the line, and the tooltip. popup(widget) points it at the button that was clicked, refreshes the lines and tooltips (a program may have been installed since), selects the kind made last, and shows it. A row activated closes it, makes the brick with factory.new_brick(kind.type, new_name(factory, kind)), selects the new row, and calls gui.curtain_up(brick), which opens the settings. The tab builds the popover once and keeps it: a popover destroyed in its own “closed” crashed the brick menu (c1deff3). gui/dialogs/newbrick.py goes.
  • rowtab.py: new(widget) gets the button clicked, the header’s or the empty page’s, so that the popover points at it. The Events tab ignores it and opens New Event as today.
  • brickinfo.py, which imports no GTK, gets a record per kind in the popover’s order: the factory’s type, the words already in KINDS, the group, the line, the text of the tooltip and the prefix of the names, the texts marked N_(). It also gets new_name(factory, kind), and issue(kind, vde_folder, qemu_folder), the line and the paragraph of a missing program, or None; the folders are the vde_path and qemu_path settings, as the check at start uses.
  • The bricks declare their programs: programs, a class attribute, a tuple of choices, where any one program of a choice is enough. The knowledge stays with the bricks, a test holds it equal to what command() runs, and a Programs window (TODO) could use it later.
  • The factory checks a brick’s name for its kind: check_name(type, name) covers today’s normalize_name() and check_socket_room(), plus a class’s own check_name(), which only Tap has. Rename calls it, and new_brick() refuses such a name too, for the console.
# bricks/tap.py
class Tap(bricks.PrivilegedBrick):
    type = "Tap"
    programs = (("vde_plug2tap",),)

    @classmethod
    def check_name(cls, name):
        # the host's interface takes it: IFNAMSIZ less the NUL
        if len(os.fsencode(name)) > 15:
            raise errors.InvalidNameError(...)

# bricks/netemu.py: either program runs it
    programs = (("vde-netemu", "wirefilter"),)

# gui/mainwindow/bricks/brickinfo.py
@attr.define(frozen=True)
class Kind:
    type: str           # the factory's: "tap"
    words: str          # N_("Tap")
    group: str          # N_("This computer")
    line: str           # N_("This computer, plugged into a switch")
    about: str          # the tooltip
    prefix: str         # "tap": tap1, tap2...

A kind remembers nothing across runs. For the length of a run, the popover opens on the kind made last, so a second machine is two keys away: New Brick, Enter.

§6Implementation plan

A commit per step. At every step the application works, and each step ends with the tests, ruff and black, and a stop for your review.

  1. The kinds, without GTK.

    The Kind records in brickinfo.py, with N_(); new_name(); programs on the brick classes and issue(); check_name() in the factory and on Tap, used by new_brick() and by the Rename window.

    Tests: every kind, its programs against its command(), the names, the issues, the tap’s 15 characters.

  2. The popover.

    gui/mainwindow/bricks/newbrick.py; new(widget) in rowtab.py; the Bricks tab opens the popover from both of its New Brick buttons; a click opens the settings; gui/dialogs/newbrick.py goes. A look on Broadway, then in the whole application: New Brick, a tap, its settings, and a tunnel on a computer without vde2-cryptcab.

  3. The documents.

    This page’s “Implemented”; “Since then” on 07 (its §10 item) and 01.2 (the module gone); the TODO item ticked; the translations.

§7Tests

  • The kinds, without GTK, in tests/gui/mainwindow/bricks/test_brickinfo.py. Every class in install_brick_types() has one kind, in one group, with a line, a tooltip and a prefix. new_name() takes the first free number and counts events and images too. issue() works on a folder without vde_cryptcab: both tunnels name vde2-cryptcab, Netemu is content with either program, the router names vde_router with no package, and a kind with everything installed has none.
  • The programs against the commands, in tests/bricks/. Each brick’s command(), built with a VdeInfo that has every program, runs one of each choice in programs, and nothing else.
  • The tap’s name. 15 bytes pass and 16 are refused, by new_brick(), by check_name() and by Rename. Other kinds keep today’s limits.
  • The popover, with GTK, in tests/gui/mainwindow/bricks/test_newbrick.py. It has a row per kind under three headers, each with its line and tooltip. A kind with an issue shows it in the line and first in the tooltip, and its row stays sensitive. Activating a row makes the brick with the next free name, closes the popover, selects the new row and calls curtain_up with it. Opening it again refreshes the issues and selects the kind made last. Both New Brick buttons of the tab open it.
  • On Broadway: the popover at its natural size from both buttons, a tooltip shown; then the whole application, from New Brick to the settings of the new tap.
